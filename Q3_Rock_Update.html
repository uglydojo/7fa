SEVEN FIGURE AGENCY
Q3 Rock Update · 14 qualified membership strategy sessions per week · Esteban Baez · September 22, 2026

Nothing was watching the number the whole rock depended on.

The Q3 rock finishes off track. The supply of qualified leads it needed had been falling for four months before anything broke, and I never saw it. I set a commitment that depended on a number I never wrote down, never agreed with the department that controls it, and never put a check on. That is the actual failure, and it is mine. This brief is the measurement I never built, and the four systems that mean I never have to write it again. The closing engine is working, the database is deep, and we now know exactly which lever is short. That is a good position to be in eight weeks before we set Q4.

What we can prove: none of those five has an ad click on their record. Four arrived direct to our website, one through branded search. Across all 18, six have a paid touch we can see and eight have no source recorded anywhere at all.

What we cannot prove, and I want to be straight about it: where the intent came from. Our tracking captures the last click before the form. It does not capture the thing that made somebody go looking in the first place.

What is highly possible, and happens constantly: a person is shown a Facebook ad, does not click it, and two days or a week later types sevenfigureagency.com into their browser or searches our name on Google. They arrive as direct or branded search. The ad did the work and the record shows none of it. No tracking system we have, or could reasonably buy, sees that.

🔴 So the honest statement is not a statement about advertising. It is a statement about visibility. On eighteen deals we can see a paid touch on six and nothing at all on eight. That is how much visibility we have on our own closes. Every paid figure in this brief is therefore a floor and never a total, and nothing here should be used to argue that our ads did or did not produce — in either direction. We do not have the data to make that argument, and the first thing I would want from a second acquisition channel is a cleaner answer to this exact question.

Why I never caught it

Four checks I never built. Every one of them was mine to ask for.

  • 01
    I measured the quality of the calls I got, and never the supply of calls to get. Qualified versus unqualified was on the weekly scorecard in front of this room every single week, and it was falling in plain sight — 75% qualified in the week I set the rock, then 43%, 67%, 63%, and 50% by Aug 24–28. That number did its job. What I never tracked is the pool it was drawn from: how many membership-qualified leads entered the business each week. A ratio tells you what happened to the calls that arrived. It cannot tell you that fewer arrived. The rock needed volume, and I was watching a percentage.
  • 02
    The rock's supply assumption was never written down, and marketing was never told it existed. 14 sessions a week implied roughly 26 qualified leads a week. Marketing had no way to know a sales commitment now depended on their volume holding, because I never gave them that heads-up. That is the single thing I would go back and change.
  • 03
    Nothing we tracked had a threshold attached to it. The qualified share fell 25 points across the rock window and I never set a level at which that became something I had to raise. Same for supply, which I was not reporting at all. A metric with no trigger on it is a metric that gets read and then scrolled past, and that is what happened for eight weeks.
  • 04
    Nothing connected an event in one department to a commitment in another. The ad-account outage was reported the day it happened and correctly identified as the cause of falling opt-ins. I never checked it against the sales rock I had set 48 hours earlier, and I was in both of those rooms.
Everything I was watching was a measure of the calls I already had. Show rate held at a 73% mean across 23 weeks with no trend. Qualified share was reported weekly and was visibly sliding. Close rate stayed strong at 47%. Not one of those is a supply number, so none of them could tell us the pool was emptying — and because the ones that looked healthy looked healthy, nothing felt wrong until I went and pulled the supply number myself.
One published number needed correcting. I checked this brief against every weekly scorecard already sent to this room. Four of the five weeks match exactly. Aug 10–14 went out as 4 membership sessions and the correct number is 5, which moves that week's close ratio from 25% to 20%. The row is corrected and the totals in this brief already use it.

The systems going in, so this cannot happen quietly again

Each one maps to a specific check that was missing above. None of them costs money.

Some context on why this gap existed at all, and why I am glad we found it now. Until May I ran sales and marketing both, so lead quality and lead volume lived in one head and the check happened automatically because it happened inside me. Handing marketing over was the right move for a company at this size, and what never got rebuilt is the version of that check that works between two departments instead of inside one person. That is an ordinary growing pain, and we are going to be a little clunky while we build it.

I would rather have found this in a quarter we can still act on than in a year, at twice the size, with twice as much riding on it. The four systems below are the version that scales past one person paying attention — which is the thing we actually needed in order to grow this.

What I recommend we do about it

We can push the pedal to the metal on the effort we already control. I would rather we added a second and a third engine — so that a single platform can never do this to us again.

My recommendation 01

YouTube ads, as a second acquisition channel

Aleric Heck of AdOutreach — who has spoken from our own stage — presented at the AI Mastermind in Miami on Sep 19, the event Josh sent me to. He showed a calendar filled entirely from YouTube ads.

"Facebook ad costs are going through the roof, and even when you have a great ad they're almost impossible to scale."

Why it fits us specifically: his custom-intent targeting runs our existing Google Search terms as YouTube targeting. We already run search ads, so the intent data exists today — people actively searching for the solution rather than being interrupted in a feed.

And our own data already points here. On the "how did you hear about us" field, members told us YouTube, Podcast, Instagram and Search Engine — channels we spend nothing on. Edwin Vincent, who tracks as a Facebook lead, told us he found us on YouTube. People are finding Josh organically and converting; this puts spend behind intent that is already there.

Watch it yourself — his presentation is here. His full strategy breakdown is at adoutreach.com/gift.

The ask: approve a scoping conversation with his team. I would come back with a proposed test budget and a defined success measure, and you would decide the spend then.

My recommendation 02

Cold outbound on infrastructure we own

This is already my second rock. Separate domains and mailboxes, never our primary sending domain, run by a dedicated cold email team.

It is the only one of our options that adds new qualified supply rather than recovering supply we used to have — and it is the only channel where an outside platform cannot switch us off.

The ask: approve a scoping conversation with Lead Gen Jay's team. Pricing comes through their process, so I would come back to this room with a real number and you would make the spend decision then. Today I am only asking for permission to go and get the number.

I have no commercial relationship, referral fee, affiliate code or arrangement of any kind with Aleric Heck, Lead Gen Jay, or any other speaker or vendor named in this document or in the Miami debrief. I attended their sessions at the event and that is the entire extent of it. I am recommending them because they are the two channels I think close our supply gap, and for no other reason.

Three rules the numbers were held to.

1. Same population on both sides of every ratio. A rate is only meaningful if the numerator and denominator describe the same set of things. The close rate counts closes that landed in the window over sessions that ran in the window — both inside the same eight weeks.

2. Two systems before any attribution claim. GoHighLevel alone reported four members as having no recoverable source. Querying Hyros resolved two of them immediately — Shawn Bell to Facebook, Travis Ventrella to a Meta campaign plus branded search. A single-system attribution answer is not a reliable one, and neither system alone would have produced the figures on this page.

3. The full record read, not a summary of it. Attribution lives in a first-source field that is separate from a lead’s tags, and several of these members appear in the system under two email addresses that the platform has merged. Reading only the summary understates paid acquisition — Edwin Vincent, Travis Ventrella and Pradnya Kulkarni all look sourceless at a glance and all three resolve to named Meta campaigns with click dates once the merged record is opened. Every one of the eighteen was read in full, including the merged duplicates.

What was deliberately excluded. Last-touch tracking data was discarded rather than used. Almost every member’s final tracked touch is our own post-purchase onboarding page, which describes what happened after the sale rather than what caused it — and order-form checkouts inherit the cookies of whoever completed the form, so two members who bought in different months carry identical tracking identifiers. Only first-touch and campaign-level data was counted.

What remains open. Eight of the eighteen return no source in either system. Two sit in legacy imports that predate our current tracking, one arrived through a third-party handoff, and one was entered into the CRM by hand. Session counts drawn from recordings are stated as floors throughout, because a held call whose recording cannot be located is absent from them. Every one of these limits is disclosed here rather than left to be discovered.
