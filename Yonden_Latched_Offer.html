1What actually happened with hemp

Not a proposal. Signed law.

A bill signed on November 12, 2025 rewrote the federal definition of hemp. Three pieces hit your clients directly:

  • The standard moved from delta-9 only to total THC, which now counts THCA. That is what wipes THCA flower.
  • Finished consumable products are capped at 0.4mg total THC per container.
  • Delta-8, HHC and anything synthetic lose hemp status outright.

The US Hemp Roundtable's estimate is that this makes roughly 95% of hemp-derived cannabinoid products federally unlawful.

The clock

Nov 12, 2026
Delta-8, HHC and the synthetics lose hemp status. This date did not move.
Dec 11, 2026
The rest of the restrictions land, after a stopgap pushed them back roughly a month.

You mentioned on our call that they were "extending it by one month." That is the Dec 11 date, and you were right. What is easy to miss is that the synthetic-cannabinoid piece was not extended. So the first real cliff is about ten weeks out, not next year.

The one live variable

The White House has asked Congress to rewrite the hemp definition and delay the ban again. A regulated version, with age gates and potency caps instead of prohibition, is genuinely still on the table. I would not build a plan on it. Plan for the ban and treat a carve-out as upside.

Sources: Congressional Research Service IF13136 · Saul Ewing · Vicente LLP · The Hill
